Mutual Fund Basics: What & How

₹

What is a Mutual Fund?

Think of a mutual fund as a large financial bus. Instead of riding solo in the stock market (which requires immense research and capital), thousands of retail investors pool their money together. A professional Fund Manager drives this bus, buying a diversified basket of 50 to 100+ stocks across various sectors.

Key Takeaway: Instant diversification prevents single-stock wipeouts.

How Do You Invest & Earn?

When you invest money, you receive Units based on the current NAV (Net Asset Value). As underlying companies grow, earn profits, and increase in market value, your NAV increases. You can invest via SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) monthly or Lumpsum.

Key Takeaway: SIP utilizes Rupee Cost Averaging during market dips.

Investment Jargon Decoded

Cost Metric

Expense Ratio

The annual fee charged by the mutual fund house to manage your money (fund manager salary, research, marketing). Lower is better.

Performance

Alpha (α)

The extra return a fund delivers above its benchmark index. Positive alpha proves the fund manager's stock-picking skill.

Risk Metric

Beta (β)

Measures volatility compared to the market. Beta of 1.0 moves with the market. Beta > 1 is more volatile; Beta < 1 is defensive.

Efficiency

Sharpe Ratio

Measures risk-adjusted return. It tells you how much excess return you received for the extra volatility endured. Higher is better.

Scale

AUM (Assets Under Management)

The total capital pooled by all investors in that specific fund. Extremely large AUM in small-cap funds can make agile stock trading difficult.

Valuation

NAV (Net Asset Value)

The per-unit price of the mutual fund. Note: A lower NAV does not mean a fund is 'cheap'; returns depend entirely on percentage growth.
